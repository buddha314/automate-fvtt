# Overview

Keeps may be generated by players and are containers for resources. They may updated by the automation facility and Role-Playing elements at the discretion of the GM.

A Keep can be a large city or a single hut in the middle of a field. Memebership in a keep provides benefits on a social level the player does not actively need to seek.

Game loops that include Keeps will probably focus on an interruption of a current gathering system. The PCs will be asked to investigate a break in wheat deliveries or fish harvests. These disruptions will be module or GM determined. Once the issue is settled, the Keep will continue gathering.

Example: Wheat used to come up the river from the Valley in a regular cadence of one bushel per week. The keep needs 0.5 bushels per week and has on one hand. The GM keeps the usage clock ticking, so the players have 1.5 weeks to solve the situation before their NPCs start starving.  If, for instance, the hamlet relied on wheat to continue fishing, which they use for revenue, then revenue also stops at the 1.5 week mark.

## Keep Metrics
Some basic metrics of Keeps include
* Membership size (essentially the number of residents)
* Land area
* Lawfulness / Order. More lawful Keeps are likely to attract more merchants.

## Keep Benefits

### Examples of Keep Benefits 
* Keeps that produce feed provide accelerated healing during rests.
* Keeps that become large enough to attract Merchants provide discounted goods and supplies to members.
* Members attacked within the domain of a keep may get help from the City Guard
* Provide endless quests to obtain or defend assets the Keep manages.
* Temporary housing and shelter in the form of Inns and Guild Halls
* Access to important figures of influence

## Keep Resources

Resources such as wheat fields or ore mines may be associated with Keeps. These resources are automatically deposited within the warehouses of the Keep, regardless of distance.

Keeps can increase their available resources by expanding.

GMs or Modules may override all resource allocations. Rules can be set to relate the number of Keep

### Examples of Keep Resources
* A wheat field the produces 100 bushels per person day
* Industrial zones that produce goods quickly and cheaply. E.g. Solar panels produced by factories in industrial areas of city

## Recruiting

Keep automatically recruit various elements such as Merchants based on their fundamental characteristics or GM overrides. The key variables for automation include the metrics above and recruiting cadence.  This is the restock rate by default.  For instance, Keeps harvest resources, recruit members and restock merchants once / week.

## Keep Tiers & Evolution

Modules and Game Systems can offer a Tiered system for Keeps. For instance

* Keeps may be tranched into hamlet, vilage, shire, town, city, megacity, national or planetary based on certain metrics.
  * For instance, if only "land area" and "population" are used as metrics. Then a hamlet may be size: "under 3 square kilometers and 500 residents", a "city" may be "Between 20 and 100 square kiometers and between 1,000 and 100,000 residents"
* Keep benefits will escalate with Tier.
* We may consider multiple tiers for different metrics in the future. E.g. "city in area but hamlet in population". Keep benefits would then be tied to the Tier. Better law enforcement with higher population but more food in larger areas.
